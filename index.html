Abstract

Demonstration-conditioned policies can confuse stages when similar observations recur or when execution and demonstration proceed at different speeds. PACE compresses the demonstration into ordered multimodal tokens and uses training-only dual-edge attention supervision to expose its stage structure. At execution time, an episode-local fast-weight memory encodes realized transitions and guides cross-attention to produce a progress-aligned context. The paper evaluates this approach on LIBERO-Gen, simulated Block Routing, and real-robot Block Routing.

Method

Use execution history to find the relevant stage of a demonstration.

PACE is a closed-loop policy conditioned on one complete perception–action demonstration. It uses the current observation and the transitions the robot has already executed to retrieve the relevant interval of the demonstration, even when the same scene recurs or the robot and demonstrator move at different speeds.

Demonstration prompt encoder

At 20 Hz, each chunk spans about one second. A pretrained CLIP ViT encodes the first frame from each camera, linear layers embed the end-effector poses and gripper states, and an MLP encodes the actions. Attention pooling fuses these features into one ordered token.

Dual-edge attention supervision

During training, changes in motion and gripper state identify candidate boundaries in the demonstration and execution trajectories. Dynamic programming aligns these boundaries in order while allowing different stage durations and missed detections.

Stateful progress alignment

Each rollout starts from learned initial weights for a fixed-size, two-layer MLP. After a completed 12-step execution segment, PACE encodes the executed actions and the start and end camera observations. A local key–value reconstruction update writes this transition into the fast weights.

A gated readout from memory modifies the current observation queries before they attend to the full demonstration. The same observation can therefore retrieve different prompt chunks under different execution histories.

Unified diffusion action expert

One conditional 1D U-Net predicts an action chunk from the current observation and progress-aligned context. Training combines diffusion denoising, dual-edge attention supervision, and effective-support regularization to keep attention concentrated on relevant prompt chunks.

At test time, the policy executes part of the predicted chunk and replans after incorporating the observed outcome. The alignment and action generation use the demonstration and realized history without stage labels.

Ablation

Fast-weight memory helps attention track execution progress.

Rollout outcomes

Full PACE completes 22 of 30 rollouts (73.3%), compared with 5 of 30 for BPP (16.7%). Wrong-stage failures fall from 14 to 1, and the other seven failures with full PACE are wrong grasps.

Effective-support regularization alone and dual-edge supervision alone yield 17 and 16 wrong-stage failures, respectively. These results support combining stage-structure supervision with memory of the current rollout.
